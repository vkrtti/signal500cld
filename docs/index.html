          1 · The weekly routine

          Friday US closeAll decisions use closing prices of the last trading day of the week. Nothing is decided mid-week.
          Data refresh (Saturday morning)S&P 500, VIX, financial conditions, Sahm rule and yield curve from the Federal Reserve; prices and fundamentals for ~500 stocks.
          Core buyThe weekly amount rule sets €40–€200 for the S&P 500 index ETF. This is the main strategy.
          Return plan checkIf you run the 20% or 30% plan: stay in the 2x/3x fund above the 200-day line, move to cash below it.
          Extra profit (optional)Close last week's 3 longs and 3 shorts, open this week's from the new ranking.
          Record & reviewThe demo account books every result, so each method builds a live track record.

          You place the trades yourself at your broker; the site only decides and records.

          2 · The methods

          Weekly buy amount (€40–€200)core strategy

          Buy more when the market is cheaper than its record, less when it is near the top, never stop. Money not spent in calm weeks stays with you; nothing waits in cash.

          Money-weighted yearly return vs a flat weekly amount (price only): 1990–2026 9.24% vs 8.99%; 2000–2026 10.01% vs 9.86%; 2007–2026 12.45% vs 12.03%; 2016–2026 14.55% vs 14.01%.           Budget

          Averaged €80–110 a week; in a 2008-style crash it asks for €200 a week for over a year (~€13,000).

          Monthly market signal (BUY / BUY EXTRA / HOLD)risk gauge

          HOLD: monthly price below its 10-month average and (Sahm ≥ 0.5 or NFCI > 0), unless a panic is already easing. BUY EXTRA: 15%+ below the peak or VIX ≥ 30 without worsening credit stress. BUY: everything else. Uses only data known at the end of the previous month.

          Result: €500/month 1990–2026 (€220,500 invested): plain monthly buying €2.58M, trend rule only €2.57M, this signal €2.46M, buy-the-dip €2.31M. No timing rule beat plain buying, because cash waiting on the sidelines cost more than the crashes avoided. But HOLD months were followed by +1.9% on average over 12 months, vs +10.9% after BUY months, so it is kept as a risk gauge, not a reason to stop buying.

          Indicator board (19 readings)context

          Grouped as trend (S&P 500, distance from 200-day average and from the record, market breadth), fear (VIX, AAII sentiment), recession risk (yield curve, Sahm rule, unemployment), credit (high-yield spread, NFCI), rates and inflation (Fed funds, 10-year yield, CPI, oil), valuation (forward P/E, CAPE, equity risk premium) and earnings growth. Each gets a status from its usual professional threshold, e.g. VIX ≥ 30 panic, curve below 0 recession warning, Sahm ≥ 0.5 recession signal, high-yield spread above 5 pp stress, NFCI above 0 tight conditions.

          Valuation: CAPE and the equity risk premiumlong-term expectations

          CAPE = price ÷ 10-year average of inflation-adjusted earnings (Shiller). Average real return over the next 10 years by starting CAPE, 1881–2013: under 10: 11.3%/yr · 15–20: 6.0% · 25–30: 4.4% · over 35: −2.6% (mostly 1999–2000).

          Equity risk premium = forward earnings yield (1 ÷ forward P/E) − 10-year Treasury yield. Near zero means stocks pay little extra over safe bonds.

          Used to set expectations, not to time purchases.

          Stock ranking: value + momentumtop/bottom 50, extra profit

          All S&P 500 members with complete data (~486) are scored within their own sector, so banks are compared with banks and tech with tech.

          value = 50% × rank(earnings ÷ price) + 25% × rank(sales ÷ price) + 25% × rank(book ÷ price) momentum = rank(position in 52-week range: (price − low) ÷ (high − low)) score = 50% × value + 50% × momentum (0–100; ranks are sector percentiles)

          Loss-making companies get the lowest earnings rank. P/E under 6 is flagged as a likely data error. Value and momentum are the two factors with the strongest academic record (Fama-French, AQR), and they work best combined because they tend to move in opposite directions.

          Extra profit picks and the demo accountsimulation

          Stock leg (Extra profit): €83.33 long in each of the 3 highest-ranked stocks and €83.33 short in each of the 3 lowest-ranked (skipping flagged P/E data; longs must be profitable). Long and short balance, so the result depends on the picks rather than on the market direction.

          Index leg: S&P 500 position of €500 × |meter| ÷ 100, long if the meter is positive, short if negative, nothing if |meter| < 10. Total weekly stake never exceeds €1,000.

          Booking: open at the Friday close, close at the next Friday close. P/L = stake × (exit ÷ entry − 1) for longs, the negative for shorts. No fees, spreads or borrow costs.

          Index leg backtest: 1990–2026 monthly €4.27 profit per €1 of average stake vs €3.41 always long; 2017–2026 weekly €1.14 vs €1.30. The stock leg cannot be backtested (no free price history for all stocks); its record starts 9 Oct 2026.

          How everything was testedmethodology
          • No look-ahead: each decision uses only data available before it is acted on (previous month or previous Friday).
          • Total return: dividends reinvested (Shiller dividend yield; 1.4%/yr in daily tests). Uninvested cash earns the Fed funds rate.
          • Leveraged funds (monthly test): monthly return = L × index − (L − 1) × cash rate − (L² − L) ÷ 2 × variance − fee, with variance from the VIX. This reproduces the daily-reset drag of real 2x/3x funds. Fees 0.07% (1x), 0.6% (2x), 0.75% (3x) a year.
          • Daily check: the plans were re-run day by day on Federal Reserve data for Jul 2017–Oct 2026 with weekly decisions, as you would actually trade them.
          • Measures: yearly growth rate, money-weighted return (each euro counted from when it went in), worst fall from a peak, worst calendar year, rolling 10-year returns, profit per euro staked.
          • Known bias: the 1990–2026 data uses monthly average prices, which smooths falls; real drawdowns were somewhat deeper. Thresholds were chosen with this history in view, which flatters results.
          Risk managementbuilt in and up to you
          • Diversified core: the main strategy is the whole S&P 500 index, bought every week.
          • Budget cap: €40–€200 a week; know that crashes ask for the maximum for many months.
          • Trend exit for leverage: 2x/3x only while the S&P 500 is above its 200-day line, checked weekly.
          • Position size: leveraged plans and the extra-profit trades only with money you could see halve (or worse).
          • Shorts: can lose more than the stake; consider a stop-loss or skip them.
          • Not modelled: taxes on each sale, euro/dollar swings, spreads and broker fees.

          How to trade it, and the risks

          Long side

          • Buy the 3 stocks after the Friday close (or Monday), about €83 each. Sell them the next Friday.
          • Many brokers sell fractional shares, so €83 works even for expensive stocks.

          Short side

          • Shorting means profiting when a price falls. For private investors in Europe this is usually done with CFDs, which use leverage and borrowing fees.
          • A short can lose more than its stake: if a shorted stock jumps 50% on a takeover bid, an €83 short loses €42. Consider a stop-loss, and skip the short side if your broker or comfort doesn't allow it.

          Keep it in proportion

          • Rankings are value + momentum factors. Their edge shows up over months and many stocks; over one week, 6 stocks are mostly luck.
          • Untested historically (no free price history for all 500 stocks), so judge it by the live record above after a few months. Costs, spreads and tax are not included. Not financial advice.

          How the risk is managed

          Built into the plans

          • Trend exit: leverage only while the S&P 500 is above its 200-day average. Over 1990–2026 this cut the 3x fund's worst fall from −99% to −58%.
          • Weekly check: fewer false switches than daily (about 3 a year), but a crash inside one week is not avoided.
          • Cash when out: money waits in cash or a money-market fund, earning the short-term rate.

          Limits to set yourself

          • Size: professionals cap leveraged positions at a slice of the portfolio. Only put in a 2x/3x plan money you could watch fall by half (20%) or by two-thirds (30%).
          • Stick to the rule: the results assume every exit and re-entry is followed, including buying back right after a scary drop.
          • Product risk: leveraged funds reset daily, lose value in choppy sideways markets, and carry the highest risk ratings (check the fund's KID).

          Not in the numbers

          • Tax: every exit sells at a gain or loss; capital-gains tax on the gains lowers compounding in a normal account.
          • Currency: results are in dollars; euro/dollar swings of 10% a year are common.
          • Spreads and data: monthly averages smooth the 1990–2026 test, so real falls were somewhat deeper. The daily 2017–2026 check is closer to reality.

          How the signal works

          The rules (checked monthly)

          • HOLD: price below its 10-month average and a recession or stress warning is on (Sahm rule ≥ 0.5 or Chicago Fed conditions index above 0), unless a panic is already easing.
          • BUY EXTRA: index 15%+ below its peak or VIX ≥ 30, without worsening credit stress. Deploy saved cash.
          • BUY: everything else. Invest the normal monthly amount.

          Data sources

          • Federal Reserve (FRED): S&P 500, yield curve, credit spreads, financial conditions, Sahm rule, Fed funds, 10Y yield, CPI, VIX.
          • Robert Shiller's long-run S&P 500 dataset (1871 on): prices, dividends, CAPE.
          • FactSet Earnings Insight: forward P/E, earnings growth.
          • AAII sentiment survey; S&P 500 company fundamentals (updated 11 Oct 2026).

          Bloomberg, FactSet and Refinitiv terminals are paid; these are the free sources their analysts also cite. Market data refreshes automatically every weekday after the US close; the weekly amount, meter and demo account roll every Saturday. FactSet, CAPE, sentiment and news figures are as of 9 Oct 2026.

          Limits to know

          • No rule here beat plain monthly buying since 1990. Treat the signal as a risk gauge.
          • CAPE predicts 10-year returns, not next month's.
          • For EU investors, track the index with a UCITS ETF (e.g. CSPX / SXR8); US-listed SPY is usually not available.
          • Not financial advice. Past results don't predict future returns.
